*This essay sits primarily under the* Are we ready? *question of the practice's [methodology](/services#methodology): a partner-level evaluation framework for AI tooling decisions, three domains and ten minutes per decision.*

## The problem

New AI tools appear constantly. Vendor pitches all sound urgent. Demonstrations always look impressive. It is hard to know which tools are worth the firm's time, let alone safe enough to use on regulated work.

This guide is a repeatable process for evaluating any AI tool before it enters the practice.

**The golden rule:** *try it yourself first.* Use the tool the way your staff would, on something close to real work. Notice where you get stuck, confused, or impressed. Fifteen minutes of hands-on testing will tell you more than any product page or sales call.

---

## The three-domain framework

Every evaluation should cover three domains. A tool can score well on one and fail on another, so you need all three to make a confident decision.

| Domain | What it covers | Core question |
|---|---|---|
| **Usability** | Interface, access, reliability, integration with your existing systems | Can your team actually use this without help every five minutes? |
| **Fit for purpose** | Whether it does what it claims, on the work the firm actually does | Does it support the way the practice runs? |
| **Governance** | Privacy, data handling, professional obligations, accountability | Is it safe for our regulated context? |

---

## Domain 1: usability

Whether the tool fits smoothly into the firm's workflow: interface, mechanics, integrations, the small frictions that decide whether a tool gets adopted or quietly abandoned.

| Area | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Interface | Is it clean, intuitive, and easy for staff who are not tech-confident? |
| Login | Does it support SSO with the firm's existing identity (Microsoft 365, Google, Okta)? |
| Distractions | Are there ads, upsells, or in-app friction that disrupt workflow? |
| Devices | Does it work on the devices the firm actually uses? |
| Accessibility | Does it support staff who use screen readers, captions, or keyboard navigation? |
| Guidance | Are there clear instructions and in-tool help? |
| Reliability | Does it load quickly and stay up? |
| Integration | Does it connect with the firm's existing systems (practice management, DMS, accounting, clinical software)? |
| Cost | Is there a free trial that gives meaningful access before committing? |

**Quick test:** if a colleague with average technical confidence cannot figure it out in ten minutes without help, the tool is probably too complex for broad adoption, regardless of how impressive the demo was.

---

## Domain 2: fit for purpose

Whether the tool actually supports the work the practice does, not just whether it looks impressive in a generic demonstration.

| Area | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Real-work alignment | Does it support an actual workflow we run, or a workflow vendors hope we have? |
| Australian context | Does it understand Australian regulation, terminology, and conventions, or default to US framings? |
| Sector fit | Does it know our profession's vocabulary and obligations? Does it cite primary Australian sources where it should? |
| Scalability | Can we use it consistently without overloading the team? |
| Verification | Does the tool make it easy to verify outputs against primary sources? |
| Replacement vs augmentation | Does it enhance the work the practitioner does, or try to replace judgement? |
| Disruption | Will adopting it require process changes the firm has the appetite for? |
| Vendor fit | Does the vendor understand small Australian firms, or is the offering scaled for enterprises? |

**The test:** if you removed the tool tomorrow, would the firm lose something meaningful, or would staff barely notice?

---

## Domain 3: governance

A workable tool cannot stand without governance. Address these issues *before* deployment, not after the first incident. Underneath this domain sits an agency question worth holding onto: does this tool make it easier or harder for a practitioner to remain the one steering the work? A tool that produces beautiful output but quietly absorbs the practitioner's judgement fails on governance even if every privacy clause is in order.

| Area | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Data collection | What data does it collect about staff, clients, matters, queries? |
| Compliance | Does it comply with the Privacy Act and Australian Privacy Principles? With professional obligations relevant to your sector (LPUL, AHPRA, APES 110, RG 175)? |
| Data residency | Where is data processed and stored? Inside Australia, with appropriate sovereignty arrangements, or offshore? |
| Training use | Is firm or client data used to train the provider's models? Is there a clear opt-out? |
| Security | Is data encrypted in transit and at rest? Is the provider SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / IRAP-certified where relevant? |
| Third-party sharing | Is data shared with third parties? For what purpose? |
| Transparency | Are terms of service and privacy policies clear and readable, or are they fifty pages of unintelligible legal text? |
| Data deletion | Can data be deleted on request? On what timeline? Will deletion certificates be provided? |
| Vendor support | Is there a clear contact for privacy concerns? Is the company likely to be around in three years? |
| Bias and fairness | Could the tool introduce bias that affects clients or staff in unfair ways? |
| Auditability | Can we see what the tool did and why on any given output? |

**For regulated firms:** if you cannot answer these clearly, the tool is not ready for client-affecting work in the practice.

## A quick-start evaluation process

```
1. Try it yourself
   ↓
2. Run through the three checklists (Usability → Fit → Governance)
   ↓
3. Pilot with one workflow, low stakes, mock data only
   ↓
4. Collect feedback (one survey for staff who used it; your own observations)
   ↓
5. Reflect and decide: adopt, adjust, or drop
```

---

## The reflection cycle

After piloting, before scaling:

1. **Observe.** Watch how staff actually use the tool. Look for fluency, confusion, frustration, or workarounds.
2. **Check in.** Ask staff directly: did this help, distract, or just add a step?
3. **Assess value.** What would they have done without the tool? This reveals whether it adds real value or just feels like progress.
4. **Reflect on your own experience.** Did it save partner time, improve quality, or create new review work?
5. **Adjust and document.** Refine the approach. Record what you learned. This is how the firm builds an evidence base for future decisions.

---

## Practical tips

- **Prioritise peer reviews from other small Australian firms.** Generic global reviews are usually written for enterprise contexts. Other small firms in your sector know what actually breaks.
- **Start small.** Run a controlled pilot with one workflow before committing to a firm-wide rollout.
- **Document observations.** Note what worked, what confused staff, and any technical issues. This becomes evidence for the next decision and the next vendor conversation.
- **Iterate before scaling.** Adjust based on what you saw, not what you hoped.
- **Sit with vendor demos critically.** Demo data is not your data. Demo workflows are not your workflows. Ask to see the tool on a workflow that matches *yours*.

## A one-page decision template

```
Tool name: ____________________   Date tested: __________

USABILITY                              Score: __ / 5
Notes:

FIT FOR PURPOSE                        Score: __ / 5
Notes:

GOVERNANCE                             Score: __ / 5
Notes:

Overall: __ / 15

Decision:  [ ] Adopt   [ ] Extended pilot   [ ] Drop

Reasoning:
```

A score of 12 or above with no domain below 4 is a defensible reason to adopt. Anything else is a reason to either keep piloting or walk away.
