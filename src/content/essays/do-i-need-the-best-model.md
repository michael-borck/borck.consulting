*This essay sits primarily under the* Are we ready? *question of the practice's [methodology](/services#methodology) — the procurement question partners ask, with a smaller answer than the vendors suggest.*

## The short answer

No. Not for most of what your firm is doing. The reason comes back to a distinction you have probably already met: **conversation versus delegation.**

If your team is **delegating** — handing AI a task and expecting a polished result — then yes, model quality matters. Precision matters. You need the output to be right the first time because nobody is planning to iterate. In that mindset, the gap between free-tier and premium feels enormous.

If your team is **conversing** — thinking with AI, exploring ideas, iterating through drafts — then a smaller model that is occasionally wrong is still useful. The team is not extracting answers. They are exploring. The back-and-forth corrects for the model's weaknesses *because the human is in the loop doing the thinking.*

The procurement question and the practice question are the same argument from different angles.

---

## Free-tier tools are enough for conversation

For conversation-style use, free-tier and bundled AI is genuinely capable:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot** — if your firm is on M365, you may already have this paid for; check your licensing
- **Gemini for Workspace** — same logic if you are on Google Workspace
- **ChatGPT (free tier)** — capable enough for most exploratory work; *not* for client data
- **Claude (free tier)** — strong reasoning, good for structured thinking; *not* for client data
- **Google Gemini (consumer)** — competitive capability at the free tier; *not* for client data

For non-sensitive, non-identifiable work — brainstorming a meeting agenda, structuring an internal SOP draft, generating discussion prompts, exploring an unfamiliar area — these tools can brainstorm with you, challenge assumptions, and push thinking further. That is what conversation requires. The model does not need to be at the frontier for that purpose.

For client-touching work, the question is not which model is best. It is which tool sits inside an environment your professional obligations allow. (See [Why Most AI Data Governance Policies Get It Wrong](data-governance.qmd).)

## When premium tools are justified

Premium subscriptions (Claude Pro, ChatGPT Plus, ChatGPT Team, Microsoft 365 Copilot at the right tier) buy you:

- **Longer context windows** — sustain a complex matter or document across many turns without losing track
- **Fewer interruptions** — no rate limits breaking flow mid-thought
- **Better reasoning on complex tasks** — multi-step problems, nuanced analysis, sustained iteration
- **Faster responses** — less waiting, more momentum

Notice what these are: they are all **conversation quality improvements.** They make the back-and-forth richer and longer. They do not change the fundamental dynamic — your team is still doing the thinking, making the decisions, iterating.

The right way to read a premium plan is as a **workflow question, not a status question.** Premium tools are justified when the work involves sustained, complex thinking sessions or sensitive material. Not because better tools produce better practitioners.

For most regulated small firms, the most defensible procurement decision is: a properly licensed enterprise tier of whatever you are already on (M365 or Workspace), supplemented with vendor-embedded AI in your practice management or accounting software, and a small allowance for staff exploration with mock data on consumer tiers. The shopping list is shorter than the vendor pitches suggest.

## "It's fine for you — you're on the top plan"

You will hear this from staff, often half-joking. Here are honest, defensible responses:

**The full version**

> The subscription buys a better conversation partner, not a better answer machine. The thinking still has to happen. Premium just raises the ceiling on what you can explore. The approach is the same on the free tier; the context window is shorter.

**The concise version**

> Conversation amplifies thinking. A better model amplifies more. But the amplification only works if you are thinking in the first place — and that is what matters most.

Both responses reframe the challenge back to the practice point without being defensive. The tool does not define the approach. The approach defines the approach. You can delegate on a premium plan and converse on a free one. The constraint is cognitive, not commercial.

---

## Be transparent about what you use

If partners use premium AI tools but staff are on bundled or free tiers, own that openly:

- *Here is what I use and why* — sustained iteration, long context, complex multi-step matters
- *Here is what I am asking you to use and why* — capable enough for the work; no incremental procurement risk
- *Here is what the difference is and is not* — longer conversations before the model loses context; the underlying judgement is the same

This transparency is more valuable than pretending the firm is on the same tools throughout. It models the reflective practice you want from juniors.

---

## The reframe

The "must have the frontier model" assumption is downstream of the delegation mindset. If you are delegating, you need precision. If you are conversing, you need a willing thinking partner — and free or bundled-tier models are willing enough.

The anxiety of "we cannot afford the good tools" dissolves when you realise what "good enough" actually means for the work at hand. For exploring ideas, challenging thinking, iterating on drafts, and developing your team's judgement? The tools you already pay for are more than good enough.

A useful sanity check: agency does not come from the model. It comes from the practitioner. A frontier model does not make a junior solicitor a better thinker, and a free-tier model does not stop a senior partner from being one. The tooling decision is downstream of the practice decision.

Start with what is available. The approach matters more than the subscription.
