*This essay sits primarily under the* How do we do AI? *question of the practice's [methodology](/services#methodology) — why chains of AI tasks compound errors rather than catching them, and what to do instead.*

## The danger of unchecked chains

There is an alternative to conversation, and it is increasingly popular. Instead of pushing back and iterating, your team chains tasks together: *"Summarise this matter. Good. Now using that, draft a letter to the client. Good. Now using that, draft a memo to the partner. Good. Now using that, draft the advice."* Each step builds on the last. No pushback, no verification, no course correction. Just a sequence of delegated tasks.

This is the most dangerous way for a regulated practice to use AI.

## How errors compound

Every AI response carries some probability of error — a misunderstood nuance, a fabricated detail, a plausible-sounding conclusion that is subtly wrong. A misread fact in a witness statement. A subsection number that does not exist. A clinical dose that is plausible but not current. A treatment of a CGT event that is *almost* right.

In a conversation, you catch these errors because you are engaged. You notice when something does not match your experience. You push back. You correct course.

But in an unchecked chain, errors compound. The hallucination at step two becomes an assumption at step three, a foundation at step four, and a confident conclusion at step six. Each step builds faithfully on what came before, and what came before was wrong. The AI is not drifting randomly. It is drifting *confidently*, constructing an increasingly detailed edifice on a flawed foundation. By the end you have something that looks thorough, reads well, and is built on sand.

This matters more in regulated work than almost anywhere else. The same fluency that makes the output persuasive to your client makes it persuasive to your staff member, who passes it up the chain to a partner who is reading the *seventh* draft and cannot easily see the original mistake. Polish accumulates while accuracy decays.

## The conversation loop as defence

The conversation loop prevents this. Every iteration is a checkpoint. Every pushback is a correction. Every time someone in your firm says "that does not seem right" or "explain why you chose that approach," they are keeping the work anchored to reality. They are not just improving the output. They are preventing the accumulation of errors that would make the output worse than useless — because *useless* is harmless, and *plausibly wrong* is what gets a firm in front of a regulator.

This is what it means to be the human in the loop. Not a rubber stamp at the end of an automated process. Not a partner who reviews the final advice and hopes for the best. An active participant at each stage, whose judgement, expertise, and critical eye are woven into the work as it develops.

## The choice

The choice is straightforward. Your team can chain tasks and amplify the hallucinations. Or they can stay in the conversation and amplify their thinking. The process looks similar from the outside — both involve multiple exchanges with AI. The difference is whether anyone is *thinking* or just clicking.

Underneath that difference is a more serious one. An unchecked chain is not just a quality problem; it is a quiet transfer of agency. Each step where nobody pushes back is a step where the practitioner becomes the editor of the model's work rather than the author of their own. By the time the chain produces a confident, plausible final output, there is no human position underneath it — only a sequence of accepted drafts that nobody can fully defend.

In a small firm, the partner sets the standard. If the partner accepts third-generation drafts without going back to the source, the rest of the firm will too.

## The loop at a glance

- **Brainstorm** — define the real question, not the attempted solution.
- **Ideate** — go wide, explore angles you had not considered.
- **Iterate** — push back, refine, redirect.
- **Amplify** — make the result yours, anchored to source material your firm can actually verify.
- **Repeat** — loop again when the thinking has not landed.
